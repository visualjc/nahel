Decided by resolving grilling ticket 6efeqsmd, charting: Wayfinder-style planning works at three altitudes with granularity to match: (1) roadmap shaping — 5k ft, create/edit/move roadmap nodes like a mind map without going deep; (2) ideation — 10k ft, 'wouldn't it be cool if' PO/CEO-level brainstorm of new features; (3) feature definition — PRD-level, how a feature works and its sub-features. Each altitude gets the partner posture: grills the human for decisions only they can make, runs research/prototype tickets AFK and returns with findings, makes delegated judgment calls, and hands off to build the moment the human says 'enough to start' — no full de-fogging required.

What happens at 'enough to start', and how does a PRD get written while sub-features are still foggy?

Rationale:
The closed-delta doctrine already ratified in Phase 4 does the planning work here: a PRD with a load-bearing foggy foundation is waterfall in disguise, deferred to an implementing agent who will guess. Foggy sub-features are never lost — they are either the next delta or a smaller cut. Build starts on the settled slice while fog keeps clearing in the background; with parallel agents, multiple deltas can be in flight. Handoff reuses prd-new -> prd-parse -> epic-decompose unchanged, pre-loaded from the map. Matt Pocock's flow deletes specs after implementation; ours archives (the human re-confirmed), because the archived PRD plus journal pointers is what a future research agent summarizes from.
